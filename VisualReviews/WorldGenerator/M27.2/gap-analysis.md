## Three sources of sand appearance

The procedural control uses the M27.1 wind, mineral, roughness and ripple system, with the two pre-existing photographed fine-detail samples neutralized. The scan control uses Poly Haven **Sand 02** diffuse, DX normal and roughness at the documented 2.14 m width. Two rotated samples soften repetition. The hybrid keeps the wind direction and dry mineral colour and transfers scan-scale luminance, attenuated normals and roughness in the near field. Stronger blending on the windward surface and fading toward the slip face and distance prevent a uniform gritty appearance. The pure scan is useful for judging grain frequency but its darker coastal deposits are a poor fit for an entire dry inland dune.

The source material is by Charlotte Baglioni, released under CC0. Three 2K maps were verified against the provider's hashes and imported into the private experimental project. The public gallery contains rendered comparisons only. [Sand 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/sand_02) · [License](https://polyhaven.com/license)

Quixel **Red Desert Sand**, a 2 × 2 m dry, fine, rippled scan, was also investigated. No matching installed, licensed copy was confirmed in the project or local asset library during this review. It is a candidate for a later direct comparison if available. The scan shown here is Sand 02. [Quixel listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/2f317df2-6c5e-47d0-b95d-c39666b54001)

## Geometry, contact and optical setup

The new surface is independently versioned. The optional secondary generator adds deterministic shelter-dependent wind forms above the existing terrain. It leaves the original graded bed fixed within the protected envelope. The broad pilot reached 0.61 m, with 13.7% of samples above 5 cm. In a low shot it produced repeated foreground ridges. The refined version peaks at 0.41 m and reduces that coverage to 8.1%. Shared tile samples, local evaluation and collision under the track were checked. Single dune, field and route implementations retain their previous pins.

A separate removable, non-colliding surface represents patchy blown sand beside and between sleepers. Its base samples the actual graded terrain; its feathered edges intersect the bed. The maximum visible deposit is 8.9 cm and the measured minimum vertical space below the surveyed railhead is about 0.62 m. That is a terrain clearance measurement, not a rendered wheel-flange clearance certification. The original rails, sleepers and train collision are retained. Note the small amount of burial in the diagnostic close-up: regular spacing and squared sleeper ends still stand out.

Pilot images revealed a zigzag dark streak on shallow new slopes. The streak disappeared on precisely the same terrain with screen-space contact shadows off, and was absent in unlit colour. The release uses directional shadow cascades, sky fill, local ambient occlusion and actual contact geometry instead. Daylight uses a 28° sun at 50,500 lux, 5,480 K, a 0.55° disk, captured sky and restrained fill. Manual exposure is shared; white balance is 6,000 K in the candidate. The ordinary Unreal filmic transform remains active. Sky Atmosphere and lightweight exponential fog separate distance layers. There is no added lens dirt, film grain, vignette treatment or external photo grade in the selected captures.
